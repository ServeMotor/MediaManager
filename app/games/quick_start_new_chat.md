# Media Manager - Quick Start Guide

## Overview
This is to use Media Manager to build an information mosaic, "...a way of understanding something by assembling many small pieces of information from different sources into a larger picture.""

## The Board

If you play with a physical board, there are 9 squares. Players try to form a row of 3 squares in a row with articles from 3 different sources on a common subject. 


## Game Setup

There are topic cards written by players which are mixed in a pile. The cards are shuffled and each player draws one. That is the subject of one row. Players can agree to draw all the Topic cards, or stop after 1 or 2 rounds. 


## How to Play

*Multiplayer*
Players all have access to a phone or a computer, or in another case, physical copies of 3 or more media sources (newspapers, magazines, blogs, etc). If not everyone has access, then players should share. 

Players agree on how many Topic cards will be created, and they create them together.  After the Topic cards are decided, but before they are dealt, players have 10 minutes (or any agreed time) to reach and consult online articles or physical media.

Players can agree if they will research media during the game, and if it is time-constrained, for example 10 minutes. One player will act as the timer for the group. At the beginning of every turn, after every player has played, and before the start of that turn, players have 5 minutes to read. Players can waive the time, and it can be stored for use on another turn. 

Players can choose to allow AI to be used. 

On a Players turn, they put down a marker on a square on the board and play their Topic Card. As an optional rule, players can either allow two players to have the same topic or disallow it. 

The player announces their topics and their articles that discuss the topic. The player announces each source and summarizes the articles. Minimum of 1 article required. Other Players can challenge the article or the source as false. If correct, player loses the square or a turn, they can pick. Player has to show the article source, and other player has to use their time to read the article if they want to challenge the player's summary. If original player is right, then the turn proceeds. Challenging player just loses their time for reading. 


### The Media Sources

Depending on the country players are in, or the topics they agree, players can choose any group of media sources. Another variant can be one where each player has 3 strikes to use when another player chooses a media source. So if 4 sources are agreed, each player nominates a source until 4 sources are agreed. However, only 3 strikes are allowed per player. 

### Match Results

First player to get 3 squares in a row wins. 

## Movement Rules

The 3 squares have to be in a row. Other players can interfere by placing their marker in the middle or the end of row that a player is aiming for. Players can choose any space to put a marker as long as it is within a row they are aiming for or starting to aim for. 

## Key Concepts

Players choose topics and form a strategy to find articles on that topic to place markers in a row. AI can enable fast location of articles, within agreed sources. 

Time management is key, as well as picking the right topics. Blocking players can also be a useful strategy. 


## Quick Play Example

Quick play would involve players picking topics for each other.
5 total turns with 5 questions, first player to answer 3 questions and summarize sources on their topic wins. 
Players have to research their own topics and other player topics in order to ask questions on it. 
True/False challenges still apply.


## Tips for Beginners

Pick the topic for yourself close to one that you care about, and sources that you read. Use GenAi to understand high level summary of other player topics. Time management is key so if you will ask questions, have GenAI assist.
