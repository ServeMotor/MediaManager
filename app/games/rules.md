# **Pick a Topic and Win**


**# of Players is 2 - 4 | Ages 12-65 | Approximately 60 Minutes to Play**

If *children* ages are 12 - 18
If *adults* ages are 19 - 65

Players can also be part of teams


---

## **Objective**

**To Put 3 Markers down in 3 consecutive squares in a row**

For the board game, put a marker down in a row of squares. The board is 9 squares.

---

## **Components**

* **What are the game items that will be used?**

Marker Tokens

Game Board

Topic Cards

Takeover Marker Token

Timer App on a smart phone or kitchen timer

True/False Card

Media Sources List

Strike Cards


* **What do they mean?**

* **Items**

*Marker Tokens* - A marker token is placed by a player on a square to claim it as part of their row. These can be colors or shapes.

*Game Board* - Game board is 9 squares with 3 x 3 arrangement

*Topic Cards* - Total number is 4 per player. Players choose the topics, if playing from scratch. Topics can be anything that could be researched in the media.

*Takeover Marker Token* - Players allowed 3 takeover markers each. It allows a player to takeover a square from another player.

*Timer* - App on a smart phone or kitchen timer as a method of keeping time. Game can be time limited.

*True/False Cards* - Players can vote on whether a player topic summary is true or false in their view. 

*Media Sources List* - Players can choose a list of media sources to use for topic research.

*Strike Cards* - Players can use up to 3 strike cards to disallow Topics or Media Sources.

## **Mechanics**

**What mechanics does the game have?**

**Examples**

1. Does every round represent the passage of time, like a day?

It is just 1 turn, which covers all players deciding what action to take

2. Do the players gamble on outcomes?

No, there is no gambling on the outcome.

3. Are there Questions and Answers?

Players talk about articles on the Topics they have picked the card on, in order to take a square. Other players can challenge the facts of the article but they use their turn to do so.

4. Do players have cards they have to play?

Players have Topic cards that, if they want to claim a square on their turn, have to be played in order to talk about an article on the Topic. The player has to show the Topic card but they can bluff on the article topic, the source and the date of the article. 

5. What decisions do players have to make?

Players have to decide how to use their time to research articles on their Topic Cards, as well as how much time to research their opponents topics. 

Player can decide to change their Topic cards each round. They must show them at the beginning of each turn. 
Players can decide how to spend their turn, to play their card, or challenge another player's article explanation. 
Players have 30 min of Time to spend on research.
- 10 min at the beginning of the game. 
- Then each round, players vote how much time to spend on research on that round.
- A challenge automatically takes 5 min from a player
- Players can "save" their time in a round and not research. 

At the beginning of the game, players choose Topics for the Topic card pile. 12 Topics are the maximum, and players decide if duplicates are allowed. If all Topics are objected to, then players randomly choose 1 Topic each from the pile at the beginning. The topic can be exchanged at the beginning of each round.

Players also nominate media sources for the articles. 4 sources are allowed.

Players have 3 strikes to use for either the media sources or topics. To approve a topic or media source, the players vote with majority voting. However, a strike disallows the chosen topic or media source.

Players can choose to take over another player's square but it costs, in addition to picking an article on the topic of the player that has the square, 1 turn, and 5 minutes from the takeover player's time.

6. Is there conflict between players?

In addition to being first to get 3 squares in a row, players can decide to block another player by picking a square in their row. Opposing players can try to take over a square one player already occupies. 

There is also the strategy of bluffing when talking about an article, and tricking opposing players into declining a challenge.

Players can also try to use time to their advantage by trying to limit other players use of time, or picking difficult topics or media sources.

7. How do players get points?

Players win by claiming 3 squares in a row on the board.

## **Details of Mechanics**

### 1. Mechanic 1

Playing cards including Topic cards. These are shared face up at the beginning of a player's Turn. 

### 2. Mechanic 2

Bluffing about article topic and media source and date. Bluffing to take a square or convince a player to waste a challenge.

### 3. Mechanic 3

Time usage - allocating time for research or trying to waste other players time.

### 4. Mechanic 4

Depending on number of players and if there is a board, players can collaborate to block players who seem close to winning or getting their 3rd square in a row.

## **Reveals & Resolution**

**If any mechanics involve hiding or competition, how do they get resolved?**

If a player bluffs about an article, and is challenged, the player has to show the article.

Players can use GenAi to find articles. 

If there is a dispute about the meaning of an article, other players vote on whether the challenge succeeds. if there is a tie, then the challenge fails.

### Outcomes

**Explain outcomes of a players choices**

Players claim a square or can't claim a square.

Players can challenge topics and article claims

Players manage their time for research and challenges



---

## **End of Game**

The game ends immediately when **describe when players win**.

The winning player or team claims the final square by successfully putting down the marker and explaining the article and source of the Topic. Any challenges are defeated, and there is still sufficient time. 

---

## **Scoring**

**How do players score in the game?**

They get squares on the board. 

---

## **Tie-Breaker**

**Is there a tie-breaker?**

If there is a question about an article or challenge, then the players vote. In the case of a tie, the square is not claimed and the challenge wins.

## **Player Comfort**

**Players have any risk of feeling psychological stress or discomfort?**

It is a game to encourage speaking about Topics and challenging on Topics. It calls for learning and processing information quickly from different sources. Players learn by failing as much as succeeding so it should be stressed that playing is a benefit and losing doesn't mean anything about intelligence or being smart. 

This game can encourage talking about Topics players are interested in and challenging articles and sources. It has a focus on gathering information and presenting it, which with GenAI can be done quickly. The game can encourage how to use information and achieving a goal while still having competitive fun.
