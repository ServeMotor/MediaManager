# Glossary

This glossary defines key terms used throughout **rules.md**, **CHANGELOG.md**, and **WHY_THIS_CHANGED.md**. These definitions are authoritative and intended to eliminate ambiguity during gameplay and rules interpretation.

---

# Media Manager Glossary

## 1. Game Vocabulary

### Block

A strategic move in which a player places their marker on a square that another player may need to complete their row.

### Marker

A player's playing piece or token placed on a square of the board.

### Penalty

A consequence imposed on a player when a challenge or other game action is successful. Depending on the rules, a penalty may include losing a square or losing a turn.

### Question

An inquiry made by a player about a topic, article, source, claim, or summary. Questions may be used to investigate information or challenge another player's understanding.

### Round

One complete cycle in which every player has had an opportunity to take a turn.

### Row

Three connected squares on the board arranged horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.

### Square

One of the nine spaces on the Media Manager board where a player can place a marker.

### Strike

A limited-use opportunity to object to or reject a proposed media source in a Media Sources variant.

### Three in a Row

Three of a player's markers occupying connected squares in a horizontal, vertical, or diagonal line. Completing three in a row normally wins the game.

### Topic

The subject, issue, event, person, organization, idea, or question that a player investigates using media sources.

### Topic Card

A card containing a topic selected by the players. A player draws or receives a Topic Card and uses its topic as the subject of their research and play.

### Turn

A player's opportunity to take an action during the game. A typical turn involves playing a Topic Card, presenting an article, and placing a marker on the board.

# 2. Information Vocabulary

### Article

A specific piece of published content about a topic. An article may come from a newspaper, magazine, website, blog, news organization, or other media source.

### Claim

A statement or assertion presented as true or factual. A claim may come from an article, a player, or an AI system.

### Citation

Information identifying the article or source being used so that another player can locate and inspect it.

### Context

Additional information needed to understand a claim correctly, such as its circumstances, timing, background, limitations, or relationship to other events.

### Corroboration

Confirmation of a claim by additional evidence, preferably from an independent source.

### Evidence

Information that supports, contradicts, or helps evaluate a claim. In Media Manager, an article can provide evidence for a player's presentation, but the article itself may need to be evaluated.

### Evidence Chain

The sequence connecting a claim to the information from which it originated.

For example:

**Player's claim → Article → Media source → Original statement or data**

Following the evidence chain can reveal whether apparently different sources are actually relying on the same original information.

### Information Mosaic

The larger picture created by assembling and comparing many pieces of information from different sources.

In Media Manager, players build an information mosaic by finding articles about a common topic, comparing what those articles say, and evaluating the evidence they provide.

### Media Source

A publication, organization, website, newspaper, magazine, blog, broadcaster, or other provider of media content.

### Source

A shortened term for Media Source. A source is where information or content originates or is published.

### Summary

A player's explanation of the relevant information contained in an article. A good summary should accurately represent what the article says without adding unsupported claims.

### Verification

The process of checking whether a claim, summary, source, or other piece of information is accurate and supported by the available evidence.

# 3. Media & Information Literacy

### Bias

A tendency to favor a particular perspective, interpretation, interest, or outcome that may influence how information is produced or presented.

Bias does not necessarily mean that information is false. Players should consider how a source's perspective may affect its coverage.

### Contradiction

A situation in which two sources, claims, or pieces of evidence provide materially different information that cannot easily be reconciled.

A contradiction is an opportunity for further investigation rather than automatic proof that one source is false.

### Disinformation

False or misleading information that is deliberately created or distributed with the intention of deceiving or influencing people.

### Independent Source

A source that provides information independently of another source being compared.

Two articles should not automatically be considered two independent sources simply because they appear in different publications. They may both be repeating the same original report, press release, interview, or wire story.

### Misleading Information

Information that may contain technically accurate elements but presents them in a way that can create a false, incomplete, or distorted impression.

### Misinformation

False or inaccurate information that is shared without necessarily intending to deceive.

### Primary Source

The original source of information about an event, statement, decision, observation, or dataset.

Examples include an official document, court filing, government announcement, research paper, original interview, company filing, or raw dataset.

### Secondary Source

A source that reports on, analyzes, interprets, or summarizes information obtained from another source.

News articles are often secondary sources, although an article may contain both original reporting and information obtained from other sources.

### Source Reliability

An assessment of how dependable a source is for a particular claim or type of information.

Reliability can depend on factors such as evidence, expertise, transparency, track record, sourcing, corrections, and whether other independent evidence supports the claim.

### Corroboration

Confirmation that a claim is supported by additional evidence or independent sources.

# 4. AI Vocabulary

### AI-Assisted Research

Using an AI system to help locate, understand, summarize, compare, organize, or analyze information.

AI-assisted research does not eliminate the player's responsibility to examine the underlying sources.

### AI Hallucination

Information generated by an AI system that is false, fabricated, unsupported, or presented with greater confidence than the available evidence justifies.

An AI may, for example, invent an article, misquote a source, provide an incorrect date, or attribute a claim to a source that never made it.

### AI Overreliance

Depending on AI-generated information without adequately checking its accuracy, sources, assumptions, or limitations.

Media Manager encourages players to use AI as a research tool without treating AI as automatically authoritative.

### AI Summary

A summary of an article or other information generated by an AI system.

An AI summary should be checked against the original source because AI systems can omit important context or introduce errors.

### AI Verification

Using an AI system to assist in checking a claim, source, summary, or other information.

AI verification can be useful, but it is not necessarily sufficient by itself. When accuracy matters, players should inspect the underlying source.

### Generative AI (GenAI)

Artificial intelligence capable of generating new content such as text, images, audio, code, summaries, questions, or other material in response to instructions.

### Human Verification

A person checking the original source or evidence rather than relying solely on an AI-generated answer.

In Media Manager, human verification is particularly important when a player challenges another player's claim.

### Prompt

An instruction, question, or other input given to an AI system to produce a response.

The quality and specificity of a prompt can affect the usefulness of the AI's response.

### Source Retrieval

The process of finding potentially relevant articles, documents, or other sources of information.

AI can assist with source retrieval, but players should verify that the sources actually exist, are relevant, and support the claims being made.

---

### Information Diversity

The extent to which an information mosaic contains information from genuinely different sources, perspectives, or origins.

Information diversity is different from simply counting articles. Five articles that all repeat the same press release may provide less diverse information than two independently reported articles.


## Final Note

If a term is capitalized in the rules and not defined elsewhere, it should be interpreted according to this glossary. In case of conflict, **rules.md** takes precedence, followed by this glossary.
